# Fast path: what's different in C++

- **Libraries.** **Eigen** is the default for real filters (fixed-size `Matrix<double, 4, 4>` compiles to unrolled SIMD code, exactly like the `Mat<N>` here but faster); **Sophus**/**Manif** for filters on rotations (the *error-state* or *multiplicative* EKF); **ROS 2 `robot_localization`** and **PX4 EKF2** are the production filters; **GTSAM** and **Ceres** do smoothing (batch optimisation instead of recursion). Python's `filterpy` and `pykalman` are the teaching references.
- **Templates for sizes.** `Kalman<N>` is instantiated per state size, so the loops over `N` unroll and the matrices live on the stack: the "never allocates" test is a design property, not luck. Dynamic sizes (`Eigen::MatrixXd`) allocate on every temporary unless you take care.
- **Beyond linear.** The **Extended** Kalman filter linearises a non-linear `f` and `h` at each step (Jacobians); the **Unscented** one propagates sample points ("sigma points") through the real functions and needs no Jacobians; **particle filters** drop the Gaussian assumption altogether. Linear-Gaussian is the case where the Kalman filter is *provably optimal*, which is why you learn it first.
- **Square-root and information forms.** The most robust implementations propagate a Cholesky factor of `P` (so it is positive semi-definite by construction), or the inverse covariance. They cost more code than the Joseph form; flight software uses them when `P` spans many orders of magnitude.
- **Tuning is the real work.** `Q` and `R` are rarely known: estimate `R` from the sensor datasheet, tune `Q` until the NIS averages ~1 (the number of measurement dimensions) over time (*consistency checks*), and log innovations. A filter whose innovations are biased or whose NIS is consistently high is telling you the model is wrong.
- **Floating point.** The Joseph form, symmetrisation and gating are all about keeping a recursive algorithm stable over millions of steps; recursive filters are the textbook case where `float` is dangerous and `double` is cheap insurance. Pair this with the compensated sums in the Data track if you accumulate.
