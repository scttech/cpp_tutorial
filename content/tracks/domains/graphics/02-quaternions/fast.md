# Fast path: what's different in C++

- **Libraries.** **Eigen** has `Eigen::Quaterniond` (`slerp`, `toRotationMatrix`, `FromTwoVectors`), **glm** `glm::quat` (`glm::slerp`, `glm::mat4_cast`, `glm::quat_cast`), **Boost.Math** `boost::math::quaternion` (which is the algebraic quaternion, *not* a rotation type), DirectXMath `XMQuaternion*`. Unity, Unreal and Godot all use quaternions internally. Note the component order differs: `(w, x, y, z)` in Eigen's constructor and Boost, `(x, y, z, w)` in memory in glm, DirectXMath and most file formats; this is a classic interop bug.
- **Storage.** A rotation is 4 floats (16 bytes, SIMD-friendly) against 9 for a matrix; compressed forms store only three components and rebuild `w = sqrt(1 - x^2 - y^2 - z^2)` (the "smallest three" trick drops the largest component), which is how networked games send orientations in 32 bits.
- **Error control.** Multiplying quaternions repeatedly (integrating angular velocity every frame) lets the norm drift: renormalise every few hundred steps, or after every step if you care; the test applies 200000 products and shows the drift is tiny but real. A rotation matrix drifts too, but *repairing* it (orthogonalising) is far more expensive than dividing four numbers by their norm.
- **Euler angles are for humans.** Convert at the edges (UI, file formats). Euler to quaternion has 12 conventions (`XYZ`, `ZYX`, intrinsic vs extrinsic); name the one you use. Gimbal lock is a property of the *parametrisation*, not of rotations, which is why quaternions do not have it.
- **Beyond slerp.** **Squad** (spherical cubic interpolation) gives smooth keyframe animation through many orientations; **dual quaternions** handle rotation + translation (skinning without the "candy wrapper" artefact); `log`/`exp` of quaternions give angular velocity (`q' = 0.5 * omega * q`) for physics integration.
- **`std::hypot`** with two arguments is exact-ish and avoids overflow; the lesson nests it for four components. C++17's three-argument overload is used in the read-only `length`.
