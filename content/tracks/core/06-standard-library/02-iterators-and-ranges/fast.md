# Fast path: what's different in C++

- **An iterator is anything that behaves like a pointer into a sequence**: `*it`, `++it`, `it == end`. Algorithms take iterator PAIRS `[first, last)` (half-open: `last` is one past the end), so they work on any container.
- **Six categories**, each adding abilities: *input* (single pass), *output* (write only), *forward* (multi-pass), *bidirectional* (`--`), *random access* (`it + n`, `it[n]`, `it1 - it2` in O(1)), *contiguous* (the elements are adjacent in memory: `vector`, `array`, `string`, pointers). `vector` and `deque` are random access, `list` and `map` bidirectional, `forward_list` forward.
- **C++20 spells them as concepts**: `std::input_iterator`, `std::forward_iterator`, `std::bidirectional_iterator`, `std::random_access_iterator`, `std::contiguous_iterator`. Algorithms pick a strategy with `if constexpr`/overloads: `std::advance` jumps for random access and steps otherwise; `std::sort` requires random access (so `list` has its own `sort()`).
- **Ranges (`<ranges>`, C++20)**: `std::ranges::sort(v)` instead of `std::sort(v.begin(), v.end())`, and **views**: `v | std::views::filter(p) | std::views::transform(f) | std::views::take(n)`. A view is a cheap, non-owning, **lazy** description: nothing is computed (or copied) until you iterate. That is why `std::views::iota(0)` (an infinite sequence) can be taken from.
- **Standard views**: `filter`, `transform`, `take`, `drop`, `take_while`, `drop_while`, `reverse`, `split`, `join`, `keys`, `values`, `iota`. (`std::ranges::to<std::vector>` collecting a pipeline is C++23: in C++20 copy into a vector.)
- **A view does not own its elements**: a view of a temporary (`make_vector() | std::views::filter(...)` stored in a variable) dangles. Iterate it in the same statement, or keep the container alive.
- **Iterator invalidation**: `vector::push_back`/`insert` may reallocate and invalidate every iterator, pointer and reference into it; `erase` invalidates from the erased position on. Do not hold an iterator across a mutation; rebuild in one pass (or use the return value of `insert`/`erase`).
- **`std::views::split` keeps empty pieces** (`"a,,b"` gives three), and its pieces are subranges, not strings: construct a `std::string` from `piece.begin(), piece.end()`.
