# Fast path: what's different in C++

- **`std::span` makes the safe version the short version.** A C parser takes `(const uint8_t* msg, size_t len)` and every `msg[p]` needs its own `if (p >= len)`. With `std::span<const std::uint8_t>` the length travels with the pointer, helper functions such as `be16(msg, p)` are easy to bounds-check once, and `subspan` fails loudly in debug builds. The rest of the discipline (check *before* you read) is the same.
- **Iterative, not recursive.** A recursive `read_name` is the natural translation of the grammar, and it is exactly the shape of CVE-2020-8622-style stack exhaustion with chains of pointers. A `while (true)` loop with an explicit `limit` variable is as short and cannot blow the stack. The same applies to JSON, XML and ASN.1 parsers: bound the depth or avoid the recursion.
- **Decode into normalised, self-contained values.** Storing rdata in uncompressed form means a `Record` can be copied, cached or re-serialized into a different message; keeping raw bytes with pointers into the original packet would make the record meaningless once the packet buffer is gone. (Production resolvers such as Unbound, Knot and c-ares make the same choice, at some cost in copying.)
- **`std::unordered_map<std::string, uint16_t>` for the compression table** is simple and fine at this scale; high-performance DNS servers (NSD, Knot) use a fixed-size open-addressing table or a trie keyed by label so that no allocation happens per name. The *rules* (case-insensitive, first spelling wins, only offsets below 0x4000) are what is observable on the wire.
- **Libraries.** `c-ares` (used by curl and Node.js), `ldns`, `dnspp`/`dnslib` for Python, `miekg/dns` for Go, `trust-dns`/`hickory-dns` for Rust. All of them have shipped fixes for the exact bugs listed in the lesson; the hostile-packet test list here is a fair summary of what their fuzzers found.
- **Why the test list has 16 shapes of bad name.** Fuzzing (libFuzzer, AFL) is how these parsers are hardened; a unit-test list derived from past CVEs is the cheap, deterministic complement, and each case in it is a regression test for a real vulnerability class.
